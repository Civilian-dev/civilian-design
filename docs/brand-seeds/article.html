Field notes

A field guide to
small libraries

14 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

How to pick, read and keep the small pieces of code that most projects are really made of.

Most software is assembled, not written. The parts that matter are small, and choosing them well is most of the job. This guide covers how to judge a small library before you depend on it, with a short checklist at the end.

In short

Read the source before the README. If you cannot read it in one sitting, it is not a small library.

01

What makes a library small?

Size is not line count. A small library does one job and says so. It can be described by an unordered list:

  • One job, named in the first line of the README.
  • No settings you do not need on day one.
  • Few or no dependencies of its own.

Or by the order you would check them in:

  1. Open the source and count the files.
  2. Read the tests to see what it promises.
  3. Check when it last changed, and why.

And these are the terms used in the rest of the guide:

Surface
Everything you can call. Smaller is better.
Weight
What it adds to an install, including its own dependencies.
Upkeep
How often you will have to think about it again.

If you cannot read it in one sitting, it is not a small library.
